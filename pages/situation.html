01 / Situation

What was the challenge?

A fragile payload, a hard floor and a deliberately tiny budget of materials. Before any design work, we had to agree on exactly what we were up against.

the scenario

A boiled egg versus gravity

Eggs are fragile. A boiled egg can break the moment it hits the ground. In the Egg Drop Challenge, our class had to protect a boiled egg from a fall using only a limited set of materials.

There is no clever shortcut here. You cannot make the fall gentler by wanting it more. Every advantage has to come from the design: slowing the descent, spreading the impact, or buying the shell a few extra milliseconds to stop.

design brief, in one line

Build a package from the issued kit that gets one boiled egg to the floor intact.

goal

Keep the egg fully intact after the drop.

Not "mostly fine". Intact — no cracks, no leaks, no hairline fractures in the shell.

team

Aayush & Kunj

Two operators: four sketches, one selection, one build.

output

  • · one package, built to spec
  • · one intact boiled egg
  • · a written record of every decision

> why this is a real engineering problem

Three problems at once

The challenge is not one problem. It is three, and a fix for one usually makes another worse.

PROBLEM 01

The shell is brittle

It resists a slow squeeze far better than a fast hit. Speed is the enemy, not load.

PROBLEM 02

Materials are scarce

Three sheets of cardstock and one arm-length of tape. Nothing else is allowed in.

PROBLEM 03

A stranger must load it

The final package has to be obvious enough for someone who has never seen it before.
