03 / Investigation

What makes an egg break?

Before designing anything, we worked out the physics. It turns out the shell does not care about height — it cares about how fast the egg has to stop.

the physics

F = Δp / Δt

Force equals the change in momentum divided by the time it takes to stop. The longer the egg takes to stop, the smaller the force on its shell.

That single equation is the entire strategy. We are not trying to remove the impact — we are trying to stretch it out so the same amount of momentum is released gently instead of all at once.

step 1 · arrive slowly

Momentum is p = m·v, so a parachute cuts the top half of the fraction.

step 2 · stop slowly

Padding and crumple zones stretch Δt, shrinking the result on the bottom.

step 3 · spread it

A wider contact patch turns the same force into less pressure on any one point.

> protection toolkit

Four ways to save a shell

Each principle attacks the problem from a different side, and the winning design used more than one of them.

PRINCIPLE 01

Security

If the egg falls out of its container, it is game over. The payload has to stay put no matter which way the package tumbles.

PRINCIPLE 02

Air resistance

A parachute slows the fall, so the egg hits with less speed. Less speed means less momentum to kill on landing.

PRINCIPLE 03

Spread the force

A bigger contact area means less pressure on any one spot of the shell, which keeps a single weak point from taking the whole hit.

PRINCIPLE 04

Crumple zones

Parts that bend or crush soak up energy before it ever reaches the egg. The package is supposed to lose the fight, not the payload.

> design space

The options we compared

The class option sheet gave us five numbered concepts to weigh against the criteria before we committed to anything.

DESIGN 01

Rigid framed case

DESIGN 02

Parachute-only rig

DESIGN 03

Padded shell

DESIGN 04

Rod suspension

DESIGN 05

Hybrid case + canopy

Every concept on that sheet solves one of the four principles well and the others badly. The hybrid design is the only one that stacks two of them.
